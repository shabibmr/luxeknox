# Luxe Knox — Personal Training Client File: Detailed Analysis

**Source:** `Luxe_knox_pt_client__file_1.pdf` (10 pages, blank template)
**Subtitle on cover:** Premium Client Intake • PAR-Q • Assessment • Program • Session Log • Progress Card
**Classification:** "CONFIDENTIAL CLIENT RECORD — For authorized Luxe Knox coaching/management use only."
**Footer brand line:** LUXE KNOX • PERSONAL TRAINING • COMMUNITY • WELLNESS • PERFORMANCE

---

## 1. What this document is

A **blank, printable, paper-based client dossier** that follows one personal-training (PT) client from first contact to periodic review. It is a form template: every field is a blank line (`____`) or checkbox (☐). It contains no client data.

It covers the full PT lifecycle in nine numbered sections plus a cover page:

| Page | Section | Purpose | Filled in by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client Cover | Identity, package, goals, preferences | Trainer/front desk with client |
| 2 | 1. PAR-Q+ / Health Screening | Safety gate before training | Client + trainer |
| 3 | 2. Health & Lifestyle Intake | Context: history, sleep, stress, nutrition | Trainer interview |
| 4 | 3. Baseline Body & Fitness Assessment | Measurements and performance markers over time | Trainer |
| 5 | 4. Goal & Program Design | 12-week plan structure | Trainer |
| 6 | 5. Workout Plan — Session Template | Per-session exercise prescription and actuals | Trainer (+ client signature) |
| 7 | 6. Session Log | Master one-line-per-session history | Trainer |
| 8 | 7. Progress Review — Client Presentation Card | Review summary handed to the client | Trainer + client |
| 9 | 8. PT Package & Attendance Tracker | Session counts, sign-in, package balance | Trainer + client |
| 10 | 9. Client Sign-off & Record Review | Formal close-out of each review period | Client, trainer, manager |

## 2. Page-by-page breakdown

### Cover — Client Cover (p.1)

**Client detail information** (10 fields):
- Client ID
- Client name
- Date of birth / Age
- Phone / Email
- Emergency contact + phone
- Membership start date
- PT package / sessions
- Assigned trainer
- Assessment date
- Next review date

**Client goals** — checkbox list, each with a "Target / deadline" blank:
1. Fat loss
2. Muscle gain
3. Strength
4. Fitness / conditioning
5. Mobility / movement
6. Sports / HYROX / running
7. General wellness

**Client preferences & coaching notes** — three free-text columns:
- Preferred training days/times
- Exercises / client preferences
- Important coaching notes

*Observation:* the "Sports / HYROX / running" option signals the club targets hybrid-fitness and race-prep clients, not only general gym members. The goal list is multi-select (checkboxes), so a client may have several goals, each with its own deadline.

### Section 1 — PAR-Q+ / Health Screening (p.2)

Disclaimer: use an *approved, current* PAR-Q+ form/process; this page is a **coaching screening record, not a medical diagnosis**. A "yes" on any question requiring further assessment means pause or modify training and follow the referral/clearance process.

Seven Yes/No questions, each with a "Details / action" line:
1. Doctor-diagnosed heart condition, or told to do only professionally recommended activity
2. Chest pain during physical activity
3. Chest pain at rest in the past 30 days
4. Loss of balance from dizziness, or loss of consciousness
5. Bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that activity could worsen
6. Medication or another health condition that may affect exercise
7. Any other reason not to participate in physical activity

Also on the page:
- **Trainer screening notes / referral required** (2 free-text lines)
- **Client declaration:** answers are honest; client will report relevant health changes; trainer gives no medical diagnosis
- **Signatures:** client, trainer, date

*Observation:* the seven questions are a condensed version of the PAR-Q+ (the official form has more questions and follow-up pages). The document itself says to use the approved current form, so this page works as an internal record of the outcome.

### Section 2 — Health & Lifestyle Intake (p.3)

**Area / Client information table:**

| Area | Format |
|---|---|
| Current activity level | Checkbox: Sedentary / Light / Moderate / High |
| Training history | Free text |
| Previous injuries / limitations | Free text |
| Current pain / discomfort | Free text |
| Sleep (typical hours) | Short blank |
| Stress level (1–10) | Short blank |
| Work/activity demands | Free text |
| Typical steps/activity | Short blank |
| Nutrition approach | Free text |
| Hydration habits | Free text |
| Recovery habits | Free text |
| Other relevant notes | Free text |

**Privacy note:** medication/supplement information should be recorded only where relevant to safe exercise and handled confidentially.

**Lifestyle priorities table** — four priorities, each with "Current status" and "First action": Sleep, Nutrition, Daily activity, Recovery.

*Observation:* this treats coaching as holistic (sleep, stress, nutrition, recovery), and the "First action" column forces a concrete next step per priority.

### Section 3 — Baseline Body & Fitness Assessment (p.4)

Guidance: measure consistently (same equipment, similar time and conditions) and tell the client that day-to-day changes can reflect hydration, food and other factors.

Both tables use four time columns: **Baseline → Review 1 → Review 2 → Review 3**.

**Body metrics:** Body weight (kg), Height (cm), Waist (cm), Hip (cm), Resting HR (if appropriate), Other agreed metric.

**Performance markers** (the specific test is left to the trainer):
- Squat / leg pattern
- Push strength
- Pull strength
- Core / trunk marker
- Cardio marker
- Mobility marker

Plus a free-text **Assessment observations** block.

*Observation:* the performance markers are named by movement pattern (squat, push, pull, core, cardio, mobility), not by a fixed test such as "1RM back squat". Trainers pick the test, so values will not compare across clients unless the club standardises them. Resting HR is marked "if appropriate", which fits the document's non-medical stance.

### Section 4 — Goal & Program Design (p.5)

| Element | Format |
|---|---|
| 12-week primary objective | Free text |
| 4-week milestone | Free text |
| Weekly training frequency | ___ sessions/week |
| Session duration | ___ minutes |
| Preferred split | Free text |
| Cardio / conditioning | Free text |
| Mobility / recovery | Free text |
| Progression method | Checkboxes: Load, Reps, Volume, Density, Technique, Other |
| Main success measure | Free text |

Plus a 2-line **Trainer programming notes** block.

*Observation:* the planning unit is a **12-week block with a 4-week milestone**, which is the same cadence as the Review 1–3 columns in Section 3. The progression options (load, reps, volume, density, technique) are the standard progressive-overload levers.

### Section 5 — Workout Plan: Session Template (p.6)

**Header:** Trainer, Client, Session #, Date, Goal.

**Exercise grid (8 rows):** Exercise | Sets | Reps / Time | Load | RIR / RPE | Rest | Actual / notes

- RIR = reps in reserve; RPE = rate of perceived exertion. Both effort scales are accepted.
- "Actual / notes" lets the trainer record what happened against the prescription.

**Other blocks:**
- Warm-up / activation
- Cool-down / recovery
- **Session rating & client feedback:** Client effort (1–10), Session satisfaction (1–10), Pain/discomfort (0–10), Trainer note
- **Signatures:** client (session completed) and trainer

*Observation:* the pain/discomfort scale runs 0–10 (0 = none), while effort and satisfaction run 1–10. A per-session pain score gives an early injury-warning signal. The client signature on each session doubles as proof the session happened.

### Section 6 — Session Log (p.7)

A master table, repeated for every PT session ("duplicate as required"). Columns: **Date | # | Focus | Key exercises / loads | RPE | Client feedback | Trainer initials**. It has 9 rows per page, plus a "Session notes / next-session plan" block.

*Observation:* Section 5 holds the full detail of one session. Section 6 is a one-line summary per session, so the two overlap. The log is the quick-scan history a trainer reads before the next session.

### Section 7 — Progress Review: Client Presentation Card (p.8)

Header: review period (from–to) and trainer.

**Measure table:** Start | Current | Change | Target, for Weight, Waist, Strength marker, Cardio marker, Other agreed metric.

**Progress highlights (checkboxes):** Improved technique, Increased strength, Improved conditioning, Better consistency, Improved mobility, Goal milestone achieved, Other.

**Free text:** Trainer's progress summary; Client feedback / achievements.

**Next phase:** Next goal | Training focus | Review date.

**Acknowledgement:** client and trainer each sign and date.

*Observation:* this is the client-facing page, and the title says "presentation". It has no hip, resting HR or mobility rows even though Section 3 tracks them. The Change column is computed by hand (Current − Start).

### Section 8 — PT Package & Attendance Tracker (p.9)

**Attendance table (12 rows):** Session # | Date | Trainer | Completed ☐ | Client sign

**Package summary:** Package purchased | Sessions included | Sessions used | Remaining | Expiry/review

**Trainer handover / continuity notes** (2 lines) — for when the client changes trainer.

*Observation:* 12 rows suggests a 12-session pack (or a monthly 3×/week pack) is the typical unit. The "Expiry/review" field means packages expire. The "Sessions used" and "Remaining" counts are kept by hand and could drift from the signed attendance rows.

### Section 9 — Client Sign-off & Record Review (p.10)

The trainer walks the client through recorded results at the end of each agreed review period.

**Checklist ("Discussed"):** Goals reviewed, Measurements reviewed, Performance reviewed, Attendance/consistency reviewed, Program updated, Next goals agreed.

**Final review notes:** 3 lines.

**Signatures:** Client | Trainer | Manager/Head Coach (if required) | Date

**Confidentiality & record keeping:** access limited to authorised Luxe Knox personnel for coaching/administrative purposes; store securely, restrict access, follow privacy and data-retention requirements.

## 3. The client journey the form implies

```
Cover (identity, package, goals)
   → PAR-Q+ screening  ── "yes" answer → pause/modify, referral or clearance
   → Lifestyle intake
   → Baseline assessment
   → Goal & program design (12-week plan, 4-week milestone)
   → Repeating loop:  Session plan (5) → Session log (6) → Attendance tick (8)
   → Review (every ~4 weeks): Assessment columns (3) → Progress card (7) → Sign-off (9)
   → Next phase: goals re-set, program updated, back to the loop
```

---

## 4. Key themes and design principles

1. **Safety first.** Health screening comes before any training, with an explicit pause/refer rule. Pain is scored every session.
2. **Not a medical record.** The document says three times that it is not a diagnosis. Medication data is limited to what is needed for safe exercise.
3. **Measure, review, re-plan.** Baseline plus three reviews, a 12-week objective with a 4-week milestone, and a formal sign-off each period.
4. **Accountability on both sides.** Client and trainer sign each session, the declaration, the progress card and the final review. A manager signs when required.
5. **Holistic coaching.** Sleep, stress, nutrition, hydration and recovery sit next to the training data.
6. **Client-facing communication.** The progress card is built to be handed over and talked through.
7. **Continuity.** Handover notes and the master log let another trainer pick up a client.
8. **Privacy.** Access is limited to authorised staff, and storage and retention rules are stated.

## 5. Data model the form implies

Useful if any of this is digitised (the repo already has PT purchase and scheduling work):

| Entity | Key fields (from the form) | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Client** | ID, name, DOB/age, phone, email, emergency contact, membership start, preferences, coaching notes | 1 → many of everything below |

Fields the form implies but does not name: a session status (completed / missed / cancelled), the trainer's identity on each exercise log, and the units for the cardio and mobility markers.

## 6. Gaps, ambiguities and risks

**Content gaps**
- No **informed-consent / waiver / liability** section beyond the PAR-Q+ declaration. This is usually separate and may need legal review.
- No **cancellation / no-show / late-arrival** tracking, although it is package-based.
- No **payment or invoice** fields (amount, mode, receipt), only the package description.
- No **photo or body-composition** consent or fields (body fat %, progress photos).
- No **injury or incident report** page, although pain is tracked.
- No **marketing consent**, **photo-use consent** or **data-processing consent** line, though the page says privacy rules apply.
- The PAR-Q+ page is a shortened version. The approved form should be attached or referenced by version and date.
- The **re-screening trigger** is only in the declaration text ("inform us of changes"). There is no date or frequency field.
- No **age / minor** handling (guardian signature) even though DOB/age is captured.

**Design and usability issues**
- **Duplication:** the exercise grid (Section 5) and the Session Log's "Key exercises / loads" (Section 6) hold overlapping data, so trainers write the same thing twice.
- **Manual arithmetic:** the Change column, Sessions used and Remaining are hand-calculated and can disagree with the rows above them.
- **Only 3 review columns** (Review 1–3) fit, which covers about 12 weeks at a 4-week cadence. Longer relationships need extra sheets.
- **12 attendance rows and 8 exercise rows** are hard caps per page.
- **Free-text performance markers** make progress hard to compare across clients or trainers.
- **Mixed scales:** 1–10 for effort and satisfaction vs 0–10 for pain. Without labelled anchors, scores vary by person.
- The progress card omits hip, resting HR and mobility, which are tracked in the assessment.

**Privacy and compliance risks of a paper file**
- Health and emergency-contact data sit on paper. The document asks for secure storage but gives no retention period, owner or disposal rule.
- Applicable law is not named (for example, India's DPDP Act 2023 if operating in India). Confirm requirements with the club.

## 7. Suggestions if this becomes software

1. Store **screening answers as structured data** with a pass / refer / clear status, and block booking or session start when status is "refer" until cleared.
2. **Derive** Change, Sessions used and Remaining from the underlying records rather than entering them.
3. Make **performance markers a configurable test library** (name, unit, direction of improvement) so results compare across clients.
4. Use **one session record** to feed the exercise grid, the log and the attendance tracker.
5. Replace paper signatures with **digital acknowledgement** (timestamp, user, device) for session completion, declaration and review sign-off.
6. Add the missing fields above (consent, payment, status, incident) after a business and legal decision.
7. Apply **role-based access** (trainer sees own clients, manager sees all) and an audit log, matching the confidentiality statement.
8. Build the **progress card** as a generated, shareable client view (PDF or in-app) from review data.

---

## 8. Summary

The PDF is a well-structured, safety-conscious coaching file. It runs from a PAR-Q+ screening and lifestyle intake, through baseline measures and a 12-week program, to session-level logging, package tracking and formal reviews. It is a blank template, so it holds no client data and no business rules beyond what the layout implies. Its main weaknesses are duplicated entry, hand-calculated totals, free-form performance markers and missing consent and payment records. Each of these is easy to fix if the form is digitised.
